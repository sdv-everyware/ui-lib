#sheet in Figma

tap or drag the component: the right side shows what happens in the app

The container for anything over the screen, Material 3's dialog and side sheet, with the app's content component in its slot.

A sheet is an instance of the library's #sheet with the app's content component swapped into its slot: see #sheet.

Three things make a sheet in the design:

1. The content: a component of the app's design (#wifi-content, #device-content, …), set to auto layout that hugs one inner frame of the content's size, with the app's layers inside that frame (#wifi/networks, #wifi/password, the buttons' slots). A step or mode of the sheet is a variant property of this component (step=networks|password). 2. The sheet on the screen: an instance of the library's #sheet (layout=dialog, or a panel edge; state=closed) in a plain frame over the whole screen. Select its nested #sheet/content and swap the instance to the content component. Rename the three parts in the instance for this sheet: #sheet/scrim → #<x>/scrim, #sheet/card → #<x>/card, #sheet/content → #<x>/content, so the app can drive each sheet by its own names. 3. Nothing hidden and nothing resized by hand: the card hugs the content (a dialog's height follows it), the closed variant keeps the card behind the screen's edge; the app slides it in.

The sheets the app has now (#wifi, #device, #panel) are built exactly like this; the designer frames below the dashboard show each one open.

layout is where the card is: dialog (1120 wide in the middle, in from the bottom, as high as its content) or a panel along an edge (left, right: half the width and the full height; top, bottom: the full width and half the height). state is closed (the card 40 px behind its edge, the scrim clear) or open. The parts: #sheet/scrim, #sheet/card, #sheet/content, a slot that holds the content component.

The app never sets a sheet's variant: harry would take the variant from the Library and the instance's own part names and content would be lost. The instance stays the closed variant of the design; the app slides the card from behind its edge and fades the scrim, both display-only.

What the code sets, and how it moves

the library's container for anything over the screen: #sheet/scrim (opacity 0 to 35 %) and #sheet/card, a dialog 1120 wide in the middle, as high as its content plus 40 around it (the card hugs the content; the app reads the content's height from the .dcf, design.rs), or a panel along an edge, half the screen; #sheet/content is an empty slot for the app's content. The app gives the card its position (FollowPositionCustomizer, ui-lib sheet.rs: the closed card waits 40 px behind its edge, a dialog is centred by its content's height) and the scrim its opacity, so open and close are display-only; the app never sets the instance's variant (the Library's variant has no content)

  1. A property, a value or a transition: components.json; the order of props is the order of the variant name.
  2. The drawing: the component set #sheet in the Figma library, every variant; the layers the code sets keep their names #sheet/<part>, nothing hidden.
  3. The behaviour: the Rust module (Sheet; variant() produces the set's variant string); the parts: the harry binding.
  4. Fetch the design into the app and run everyware_ui_test: it fails when a variant or a part the code uses is missing in the design.
Traps
  • Setting Variant on the sheet instance shows every sheet open and empty: the Library's variant has no content and the names #sheet/*.
  • A size override on the nested content instance is not honoured by Figma's import; the content component hugs a box instead (hugBox).
  • A dialog higher than the screen above the keyboard sits at the top; a panel never moves for the keyboard.
